# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: the workplace decision-maker.** A facilities or office manager, HR lead, or the owner
of a small factory, hotel, car wash or workshop in Bulgaria. They are equipping their own site so
staff and visitors have coffee and snacks. The machine is an operating expense, not an
investment. They know their headcount, their shift pattern and roughly what their people drink.
They do not know vending machine models, and they should never be asked to.

**Secondary: the small vending operator.** Rents several machines to place at third-party sites
and earns from the sales. Uses the same catalogue and the same rental terms, and typically
arrives through a multi-machine enquiry. Served, but not addressed with separate messaging in v1.

**Who the business contracts with:** companies and sole traders (ЕТ). VAT registration is
therefore optional rather than required, and the smallest customers may fall under consumer
protection provisions, so claims must meet the stricter standard rather than the B2B one.

## Product Purpose

A lead generation site for vending machine rental in Bulgaria. It has one measurable output: a
qualified enquiry reaching the owner, carrying enough context that he can answer it with a real
price for a real machine.

Everything on the site is instrumentation around that event. Success is the number and quality of
enquiries, not traffic, time on page, or pages per session.

## Positioning

**Real stock, individually priced, visible now.** Every Bulgarian competitor reviewed publishes
no prices, no availability, no specifications, and routes every visitor through a phone call.
This business holds roughly 350 refurbished machines with its own warehouse and service workshop,
so it can show the actual unit: model, year, condition, monthly price, available today. A
competitor cannot copy that without holding stock.

**Answered within one working hour.** The pan-European incumbent and the Bulgarian field both
advertise 24 hours. A single owner answering personally can beat that, and it costs nothing to
deliver.

## Operating Context

- **Market:** Bulgaria only at launch. Later expansion named as Romania, Greece, Serbia and North
  Macedonia.
- **Model:** rental with service included. The customer stocks the machine themselves; full
  service with consumables is available on individual quote.
- **Terms:** 12 to 60 months, with a buyout at a predetermined residual value at term end. Twelve
  months is unusually short for this market, where 24 to 60 is standard.
- **Service:** 48-hour response nationwide, 24 hours in major cities. Replacement machine subject
  to a reasonable repair period and availability.
- **Equipment:** used and refurbished, predominantly models the manufacturers have discontinued.
  Buyers search for the legacy names, not the current ones.
- **Enquiry handling:** the owner personally, Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 18:00. Out-of-hours
  submissions receive an automatic acknowledgement and a reply the next working day.
- **Stock control:** machines are tracked in an internal system. The site is not the master
  record; its admin publishes and updates availability only, by hand, daily, typically from a
  phone in the warehouse.
- **Currency:** euro only. Bulgaria joined the euro area on 1 January 2026 and the dual-display
  obligation expires 8 August 2026, before this site launches.
- **Shared inventory:** the same physical machine may be rented or sold, and sales are handled on
  a separate existing site. "Sold" is therefore a real availability state here.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Catalogue:** 62 models across four categories (coffee, snack, combination, cold drinks) from
  three manufacturers: Necta including Wittenborg, FAS, and Crane. Combination machines are pairs
  of catalogued models and derive their specifications from the constituents.
- **Pricing:** per physical unit, quoted across five contract terms. Deposits and multi-machine
  discounts are determined after customer assessment and are deliberately not shown.
- **Approval:** enquiries are credit-checked against company registration, history, financial
  standing and public liabilities before an offer is made.
- **v1 scope (frozen):** home, catalogue with live availability, rental calculator, machine
  recommender, enquiry form, About, Contact, FAQ, legal pages, two to three case studies, brand
  identity, and an admin panel for machines and enquiries.
- **Deferred to v2:** buy-versus-rent calculator, machine comparison, favourites and bulk
  enquiry, coverage map, admin statistics, video.
- **Explicitly a separate product:** full machine lifecycle management (service history, repairs,
  parts, rental periods, contracts, customers, payments). Not part of this site.
- **Undecided at time of writing:** brand name, domain, final rental prices, service cost
  figures.

## Brand Commitments

**A fully standalone brand**, with no visible connection to the parent company Office Leader ML.
This was chosen deliberately over presenting the service as a sub-brand.

The consequence is recorded here so future work does not forget it: **the new brand inherits no
track record.** Ten years of trading, the warehouse and the workshop stand behind a name the site
will not use. Credibility must therefore be built from what can be shown rather than what can be
claimed.

Name, wordmark and visual identity are undecided and will be created as part of this project.
Development proceeds on a placeholder name and swaps it in at the end.

## Evidence on Hand

**Real and available:** roughly 350 refurbished machines in stock; an owned warehouse and service
workshop; over ten years trading in the vending sector; direct import of used machines from
Europe; nationwide delivery and installation with the company's own technician.

**Committed but not yet supplied:** photography of each model; photographs of the warehouse,
workshop, loading, installations and team; two to three case studies; final rental prices;
company registration details for the legal pages.

**Does not exist and must not be fabricated:** testimonials, named customers, third-party
reviews, awards, certifications, press coverage, or published performance figures.

**One specific prohibition.** The original client brief contained revenue projections roughly
four to seven times above every European benchmark, together with example calculations that did
not survive their own arithmetic. Those figures must not appear anywhere on the site, in any
calculator, or in any copy. Where the site states a number, it states a real one or it states
none.

Case studies may describe genuine deliveries, sales and service work as exactly what they were,
with customer consent, including anonymised as "a manufacturing plant in Plovdiv". Invented
customers are not an option.

## Product Principles

1. **The enquiry is the product.** Every surface is judged by whether it produces one, and the
   quality of the context it carries.
2. **Specificity is the trust strategy.** With no inherited reputation, exactness substitutes for
   reputation: this exact machine, this year, this condition, this price, this defect
   photographed. Vagueness reads as concealment to anyone buying used equipment.
3. **Never claim what cannot be honoured.** Promises are sized to what one person working
   weekdays can actually deliver. A kept modest promise beats a broken ambitious one.
4. **Availability degrades safely rather than lying.** When stock data goes stale, the site shows
   less, never something false. "Call to check" costs a phone call; "available" when it is not
   costs the customer.
5. **Compute, do not interrogate.** Ask what the visitor knows (headcount, shifts, what people
   drink) and derive what they do not (daily volume, machine count, suitable models). Showing the
   assumption and letting them correct it is what separates a tool from a questionnaire.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard has been established. Schools and hospitals appear in the target
venue list, so public-sector buyers may impose requirements; WCAG 2.2 AA is the working default
until told otherwise.
