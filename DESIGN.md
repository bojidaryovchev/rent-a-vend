# Design System: Вендинг под наем

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Service Bay"**

A well-run workshop with the lights on. Everything is labelled, everything is where it should
be, and nothing is dressed up. The product is fifteen-year-old refurbished equipment sold to
people who will inspect it, so the design's job is to look like somewhere competent work happens
— not like a café, and not like a brochure.

The world is built from three materials: **graphite** for the machines and the structural bands,
**warm paper** for everything you read on, and **safety yellow** for the one thing you should
act on. Safety yellow is the colour of machine labels, floor markings and power-on indicators.
It is native to the domain in a way that a soft blue or a coffee brown is not, and it is
unclaimed in this market.

The confirmed anti-reference is the Bulgarian vending field itself. Six competitor sites were
captured and reviewed: all six lead with a category name over stock photography, none publishes a
price, none publishes availability, and five have no contact form at all. Every rule below exists
to break one of those habits. The closest direct competitor's site carries a 2008 copyright.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Graphite, warm paper, safety yellow. No third accent.
- **Very tight radii (1-4px).** Nothing is pill-shaped; a machined edge, not a web card.
- Numerals are the hero. Prices, years, dimensions and capacities all set tabular.
- **Material, not flat.** Paper has grain, steel has sheen and machining stripe, panels have
  a lit top edge and corner rivets.
- Condensed signage lettering for display, humanist sans for reading, mono for asset tags.
- One authored motion moment on the entire site.
- Bulgarian Cyrillic set correctly, which no competitor bothers with.

**Direction history.** The first build asserted this world almost entirely through colour: no
texture, no frame, no material, one hover lift and nothing else. A review named that as the
ceiling. The world was then re-authored against exactly those gaps — the grain, the steel, the
rivets, the plate and stencil lettering and the shutter animation all date from that pass. The
palette and the north star did not change; the way they are expressed did.

## Colors

A workshop palette: warm greys carrying the reading, near-black carrying the structure, and a
single high-voltage yellow reserved for action.

### Primary
- **Safety Yellow** (`accent`): the only call-to-action colour on the site. Primary buttons, the
  demo banner, list bullets, the live indicator dot, and the rent line on the cost chart. Always
  paired with near-black text; never used for text on paper.

### Secondary
- **Graphite** (`graphite`): the machines themselves, the utility strip, the footer, the
  "what's included" band, and selected states. The material the product is made of.
- **Soft Graphite** (`graphite-soft`): a second step of the same material, for surfaces layered
  on graphite.

### Tertiary
Status colours, which exist to communicate stock and nothing else:
- **Stock Green** (`status-available`) on a pale wash — a machine you can rent today.
- **Held Blue** (`status-reserved`) on a pale wash — reserved or inbound.
- **Retired Grey** (`status-unavailable`) on a pale wash — rented, sold or in service.
- **Fault Red** (`danger`) on a pale wash — form errors and the stale-stock warning.

### Neutral
- **Warm Paper** (`paper`): the page. Warm rather than blue-white, so it reads as paper stock.
- **Sheet White** (`paper-raised`): cards, panels and the hero band. One step up from paper.
- **Workbench** (`paper-sunken`): wells, image beds and quiet callouts. One step down.
- **Ink** (`ink`): body text and headlines.
- **Muted Ink** (`ink-muted`): secondary text. 7:1 on paper.
- **Subtle Ink** (`ink-subtle`): captions, labels and meta. **4.6:1 on paper** — it was measured
  at 3.8:1 and failed AA before being darkened, and it must not drift back.
- **Hairline** (`line`) and **Strong Hairline** (`line-strong`): dividers, card edges and
  input strokes.

### Named Rules

**The Yellow Is Not A Status Rule.** Safety yellow never carries stock meaning. Availability
speaks in green, blue and grey only. If the brand accent could also mean "available", every
yellow element on the page becomes ambiguous the moment stock changes.

**The One Action Rule.** At most one yellow control per viewport. Its job is to be the obvious
next step; two of them means neither is.

**The Measured Contrast Rule.** Every neutral used for text carries its measured ratio in a
comment beside its definition. De-emphasis is a design decision; illegibility is a defect.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Oswald — condensed signage lettering, the voice of machine plates and warehouse
markings. It holds its tightness in Cyrillic where most condensed faces break down.
**Body Font:** Commissioner — a humanist sans that reads long-form Bulgarian without fighting it.
**Mono Font:** JetBrains Mono — stock references, asset tags, breadcrumbs. Never body text.

All three carry real cyrillic subsets. That is not a preference: a Latin face with Cyrillic
bolted on draws в г д и к л п ц ш щ wrong, and this is a Bulgarian site.

**Character:** Three registers doing three jobs, which is the point. Condensed signage carries
the display and the numbers, because that is what the domain actually uses — machine plates,
warehouse racking, floor markings. Commissioner carries the reading, because condensed type is
hostile to a paragraph of Bulgarian. Mono carries anything that is an identifier rather than a
word. The first build ran one family for everything and a review named it as the ceiling: weight
contrast alone was doing work that a register change does better.

Display sizes are set tight so large Cyrillic headlines hold together as shapes rather than
drifting apart into letters.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (4.25rem): the price in the hero. Used once per page at most.
- **Display small** (2.5rem): section openers on Persuade surfaces.
- **Title** (2.75rem): page titles.
- **Heading** (1.75rem) / **Heading small** (1.375rem): section headings.
- **Figure** (2rem): standalone numbers — the monthly rent in a calculator, a stock count.
- **Lead** (1.1875rem): the paragraph under a title.
- **Body** (1rem) with **body-lg** (1.0625rem), **body-xl** (1.25rem) and **body-2xl** (1.5rem)
  for emphasis: measure held at 60ch.
- **UI** (0.9375rem) and **UI small** (0.8125rem): controls, labels and meta rows.
- **Micro** (0.75rem, +letterspacing, uppercase): eyebrows and column headers. Short strings only.

### Named Registers
- **`plate`** — condensed, letterspaced, uppercase. Stamped-plate lettering for wordmarks,
  eyebrows and section labels.
- **`serial`** — mono, small, letterspaced. Stock references, asset tags, breadcrumbs. Anything
  that identifies rather than describes.

### Named Rules

**The Bulgarian Letterforms Rule.** The document carries `lang="bg"` and the body enables
`locl`. Bulgarian Cyrillic draws в г д и к л п ц ш щ differently from Russian, and without both
the browser renders the wrong forms. This is the cheapest possible signal that the site was made
for this market rather than translated into it.

**The Tabular Numbers Rule.** Any figure a visitor might compare — price, year, capacity,
dimension, stock count — is set with `tnum`. A price list whose digits do not align reads as
careless, and this site is asking to be trusted on numbers.

**The Eleven Pixel Floor Rule.** No functional text below 11px, footers and micro-labels
included. Only non-interactive legal smallprint gets 10px.

**The No Uppercase Sentences Rule.** Uppercase is for labels of a few words. Anything approaching
a sentence stays in sentence case; all-caps strips the ascenders and descenders we read by.

## Layout

A single centred column with three widths: `narrow` (48rem) for forms and long prose, `default`
(72rem), and `wide` (88rem) for the catalogue and page chrome. Horizontal padding is 20px,
rising to 32px from the `sm` breakpoint.

Vertical rhythm is section-based: 56px for a standard band, 64-96px for the hero. Sections are
separated by a hairline rather than by whitespace alone, which keeps the page reading as a stack
of labelled compartments rather than a scroll of loose blocks.

Grids collapse in one step. The catalogue runs 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 columns; content pages run 1 → 2.
Nothing uses a fluid multi-column layout, because the content is short-form and predictable.

Body measure is capped at **60ch**. It ran to 90-96 characters before being constrained.

**The Padding Not Height Rule.** Interactive controls are sized with padding plus a minimum
height, never a fixed height with centred text. Fixed heights look right until the label wraps or
the user scales their text, at which point the glyphs run into the edge.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is **material**, never a floating card. Three devices, and no generic drop shadow anywhere:

- **`bay-panel`** — a lit top edge (`inset 0 1px 0` in near-white), a 1px seating line beneath,
  and a wide, very soft shadow that sits *under* the panel rather than levitating it. The read
  is a steel plate resting on a surface, not a card hovering over one.
- **`bay-panel-dark`** — the same construction in graphite, for the calculator and result panels.
- **`riveted`** — two corner screws drawn with a radial gradient. The single detail that turns a
  rectangle into a panel, and the cheapest one in the system.

Surfaces still step tonally (sunken → paper → raised), and hairlines still do most of the
structural work. The shadows are there to seat things, not to lift them.

`hover-lift` adds 2px of travel plus a deeper version of the same shadow on catalogue cards.

### Named Rules

**The Seated Not Floating Rule.** Shadows sit under an element and stay soft and wide. If a
shadow reads as elevation above the page rather than weight upon it, it is wrong. No `0 4px 6px`
card shadows.

**The No Nested Cards Rule.** A tinted panel does not go inside another tinted panel. Cards
inside cards read as visual noise; use a divider or a paper step instead.

**The Rivets Earn Their Place Rule.** `riveted` goes on panels that represent a machine or a
result — the calculator, the success state. Putting screws on every box makes the device
meaningless.

## Material

Four utilities carry the physical world. They are the difference between a palette and a place.

- **`paper-grain`** — two radial-gradient dot fields at 14px and 9px, offset from each other, at
  3.5% and 2.5% opacity. Invisible until you look for it; the page stops reading as a flat hex
  value.
- **`steel`** — a vertical sheen (light at the top, dark at the foot) over a 3px repeating
  machining stripe. Used for the hero and the utility strip.
- **`hatch`** — 45° graphite stripes, for edges and empty states.
- **`caution-tape`** — 45° accent-and-graphite hazard striping. **Reserved for the demo banner.**
  It is honest signage: it says "work in progress" in a language everyone reads without reading.
  Using it decoratively would spend the one place the site is allowed to shout.

## Shapes

Corners are **machined, not rounded**: 1px on the smallest elements, 2px on chips and badges,
3px on cards, 4px at the very largest. Buttons carry no radius at all. **Nothing is pill-shaped
and nothing is circular except status dots and rivets.** A 6px corner already reads as a web
card; at this scale the eye registers an edge that has been cut, not a shape that has been
softened.

Borders are hairlines, one pixel, in one of two weights. Selected and pressed states swap fill
rather than adding a ring, which keeps the geometry stable as things change state.

The recurring silhouette is the machine glyph: a graphite rectangle with a lit top row and a
delivery bay at its foot, drawn at each machine's real width-to-height ratio and real tray count.
A single repeated glyph across a catalogue page undercut the claim the page exists to make.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Shape:** square. No radius, ever.
- **Accent (primary):** safety yellow on graphite text, a graphite hairline, an inset highlight
  along the top edge and a hard **3px offset shadow in graphite**. On hover it travels 2px into
  that shadow; on press, the full 3px, so the shadow closes to zero. The control is physically
  depressed rather than tinted — a soft blurred shadow here would read as a floating web card and
  undo the material the rest of the system is built from.
- **Outline:** transparent with a graphite hairline, filling to solid graphite on hover.
- **Ghost light:** for steel and graphite grounds. A pale hairline that warms to accent rather
  than filling, so it never punches a white hole in a dark band.
- **Solid dark:** graphite fill for secondary actions on paper.
- **Sizes:** minimum heights 36 / 44 / 48px, with type at 12 / 13 / 14px. Sized by padding plus a
  minimum height, never a fixed height.
- **Disabled:** `not-allowed` cursor at reduced opacity. Disabled controls that must remain
  readable use an explicit sunken fill instead — opacity alone produced 1.3:1.

### Chips
- **Style:** hairline border, 2px corners, count set tabular beside the label.
- **State:** selected chips invert to solid graphite. Chips with no results are disabled and stay
  visible, so the filter's shape does not change as stock moves.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 3px. See Shapes.
- **Background:** `bay-panel` — a machined top edge and a wide, soft shadow that sits *under* the
  panel rather than levitating it.
- **Shadow Strategy:** material, not elevation. See Elevation & Depth.
- **Border:** hairline, always.
- **Rivets:** `riveted` adds corner rivets to panels that should read as fixed to the page.
  Used on the catalogue card body, the empty-filter state, the enquiry confirmation and the home
  closing panel. Not decorative — it marks a panel as a fitted component rather than a floating
  container.
- **Internal Padding:** 16px on cards, 28-48px on panels.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** paper-raised, strong hairline, 2px corners, 48px tall.
- **Focus:** the border darkens to ink. No glow.
- **Error:** the border turns fault red and a message appears beneath, in red, at 14px. Client
  validation runs against the same shape the server enforces, and the server still validates
  independently — anything the client checks can be bypassed.

### Navigation
- A graphite utility strip carrying the response promise and phone, then a paper bar with the
  wordmark, links and one yellow action.
- Links are muted ink, darkening to ink on hover. The active section is marked with a yellow dot,
  not a background.
- Below `lg`, the bar collapses to a full-screen overlay. Not a panel offset below the header —
  the header's height changes with the demo banner, and a magic number would drift.

### Placeholder Media
The signature component. Machines have not been photographed yet, and the manufacturers have
withdrawn the product pages for most of this catalogue, so a drawn stand-in is used instead of a
stock photograph or an image belonging to a competing dealer. It carries a visible label saying
what it is, and it is derived from each machine's real dimensions so no two are identical.

### Availability Badge
A small tinted chip with a dot and a label, in the three status tones. Its text is generated by
the availability engine, never hand-written, so the label and the underlying state cannot
disagree — including the degradation to "Проверете наличност" when the data goes stale.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** lead a page with the offer and a number. "Вендинг машина под наем от 70 €/месец" beats
  "Вендинг машини" on every measure that matters here.
- **Do** set every comparable figure tabular.
- **Do** carry `lang="bg"` and `locl` so Bulgarian letterforms render correctly.
- **Do** size controls with padding and a minimum height.
- **Do** show a missing value as "няма данни" rather than hiding the row. A visible gap is
  information; a shortened table pretending to be complete is not.
- **Do** publish the measured contrast ratio beside any neutral used for text.
- **Do** keep the body measure at 60ch.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use safety yellow for anything that could be read as a status.
- **Don't** use a generic card shadow. Depth is `bay-panel`: a lit edge and a wide soft shadow
  that seats the element, never a `0 4px 6px` lift.
- **Don't** spend `caution-tape` anywhere but the demo banner.
- **Don't** put a tinted panel inside a tinted panel.
- **Don't** round anything into a pill.
- **Don't** set a sentence in uppercase.
- **Don't** use opacity to express a disabled state.
- **Don't** put meaning in a `title` attribute. It is invisible on a phone and unreachable by
  keyboard, and the condition-grade rubric is a differentiator, not a tooltip.
- **Don't** spend the focal motion budget twice. One authored moment per surface, and on this
  site it belongs to the monthly price stepping down as the contract lengthens.
